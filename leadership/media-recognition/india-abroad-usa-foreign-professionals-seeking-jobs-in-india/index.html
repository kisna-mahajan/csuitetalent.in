Context

An American Publication on an Indian Story

India Abroad was one of the most widely read publications among the Indian diaspora in the United States. Its decision to feature this interview in February 2004 gave Anil Mahajane's observations a specifically transatlantic significance — reaching an audience of NRIs and South Asian professionals in the US who were themselves weighing the prospects of returning to or building careers in India.

What had once been an occasional enquiry from overseas professionals had, by early 2004, become a regular occurrence. Recruitment firms were receiving calls not only from NRIs seeking to return home, but also from foreign nationals with no Indian origin who saw India as a promising destination for career growth.

Key Insights

What Anil Mahajane Observed

In the interview, Anil Mahajane identified several structural developments that were driving the trend:

India's economic growth was attracting international professionals, not merely NRIs seeking to return home
Enquiries were increasing in frequency — a sign of an accelerating rather than a temporary phenomenon
Organisations in India would increasingly require leaders with global exposure and cross-cultural experience
Executive search was evolving into strategic leadership advisory, identifying talent capable of leading global operations
India's multinational presence was creating genuine international career opportunities across sectors
Reverse migration represented a long-term structural change in international recruitment patterns
Why This Interview Matters

From Brain Drain to Brain Gain — Documented in 2004

When this interview was published, the prevailing narrative about India and global talent was almost entirely focused on outflow — India exporting professionals to the West. Anil Mahajane described a very different and ultimately more consequential reality: the direction of that flow was beginning to reverse.

The reach of India Abroad meant this observation landed directly in the hands of the diaspora community in the United States — the very group best positioned to validate or dismiss it from first-hand experience. Its publication was itself a signal that the trend had moved beyond rumour into documented fact.

Over the two decades that followed, the trend Anil Mahajane described became one of the defining characteristics of India's economy. International executives, domain specialists and technology professionals now routinely relocate to India to lead strategic initiatives and global teams — precisely the trajectory anticipated in this interview, published in America, about India's future.
