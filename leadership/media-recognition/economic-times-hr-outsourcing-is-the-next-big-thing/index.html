Context

A Quiet Transformation Taking Shape

By 2004, India had established itself as the world's preferred destination for information technology and business process outsourcing. Yet within India, organisations had not yet begun outsourcing their own HR functions in any meaningful way.

Anil Mahajane recognised this contradiction — and the opportunity it represented.

From the Interview

The Observations

"HR outsourcing in India has not seen the required momentum and is limited to a trickle effect, with companies outsourcing a few selected low-end HR processes." — Anil Mahajane, The Economic Times, May 2004
"Even though across the globe companies are realising that headcount is directly related to the revenue and are outsourcing most of their transaction and administration related work, the general opinion among the Indian companies is that it is still economical to manage all their HR process internally." — Anil Mahajane, The Economic Times, May 2004
"Here people are also not very clear about what exactly manpower outsourcing is all about." — Anil Mahajane, The Economic Times, May 2004
"Currently there are no standard benchmarks available so pricing varies a lot from vendor to vendor for similar services. This gives the customer the feeling that they are not getting best value for their money." — Anil Mahajane, The Economic Times, May 2004

The interview also highlighted that public sector organisations had begun exploring outsourcing opportunities — an early signal that adoption would eventually spread beyond the private sector.

Barriers to Adoption

Why Indian Companies Were Still Hesitant

Anil Mahajane identified four specific barriers holding back adoption at the time:

  • Awareness gap — organisations lacked clarity on what HR and manpower outsourcing actually involved
  • Confidentiality concerns — companies were reluctant to share employee data with external vendors
  • Quality uncertainty — most companies were unsure about the outcomes they would receive
  • Pricing inconsistency — absence of industry benchmarks meant wide variation in vendor pricing, eroding confidence

These observations were not simply criticism — they were a precise diagnosis of what the industry needed to resolve before HR outsourcing could achieve mainstream adoption.

Historical Significance

A Prediction That Became Standard Practice

What was described in 2004 as the "next big thing" is today an integral part of how organisations worldwide manage their workforce. From start-ups to global multinationals, businesses routinely depend on specialised HR partners for:

Recruitment Process Outsourcing (RPO) Executive Search Payroll Administration Background Verification HR Technology Platforms Learning & Development Shared Services Published 2004

This interview represents one of India's earliest mainstream discussions on the future of HR outsourcing — published at the moment the industry was first beginning to take shape.
