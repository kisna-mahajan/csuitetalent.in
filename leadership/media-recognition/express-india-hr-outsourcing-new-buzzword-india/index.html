Context

India at the Centre of the Global Outsourcing Boom

By 2004, India had become the world's preferred destination for technology and business process outsourcing. Yet within India, organisations had not yet begun outsourcing their own Human Resources functions in any meaningful way.

As companies around the world focused increasingly on productivity, cost optimisation and core competencies, HR outsourcing was emerging as the next significant business opportunity — one that Anil Mahajane identified early.

The Interview

A Hewitt Associates survey, "Outsourcing in the Asia-Pacific", confirmed that many companies in the region were either unfamiliar with the process and procedures of HR outsourcing, or unaware of the players operating in the area.

Anil Mahajane outlined where HR outsourcing could be applied:

"HR outsourcing can happen in areas such as payroll, employee benefits administration, fixed assets administration, network, receivable and logistics management, hardware maintenance, telemarketing, call centres and database management. In India, the most common processes outsourced are related to training, payroll processing, surveys, benchmark studies and statutory compliance."

Experts identified the basic reasons hampering growth as confidentiality and cost factors — alongside fears of losing control over data, ethics and quality concerns, and overall confidence in vendors.

Key Insights

Observations from Anil Mahajane

"HR outsourcing in India has not seen the required momentum and is limited to a trickle effect, with companies outsourcing a few selected low-end HR processes." — Anil Mahajane, Express India, May 2004
"Even though across the globe companies are realising that headcount is directly related to the revenue and are outsourcing most of their transaction and administration related work, the general opinion among the Indian companies is that it is still economical to manage all their HR process internally." — Anil Mahajane, Express India, May 2004
"Here people are also not very clear about what exactly manpower outsourcing is all about." — Anil Mahajane, Express India, May 2004
"Most companies are not sure about the end result, which they will receive from the vendor." — Anil Mahajane, Express India, May 2004
"Currently there are no standard benchmarks available so pricing varies a lot from vendor to vendor for similar services. This gives the customer the feeling that they are not getting best value for their money." — Anil Mahajane, Express India, May 2004
Barriers to Adoption

Why Indian Organisations Were Still Hesitant

Anil Mahajane identified five core challenges holding back adoption:

  • Awareness gap — organisations lacked clarity on what HR and manpower outsourcing actually involved
  • Confidentiality concerns — reluctance to share employee data with external vendors
  • Quality uncertainty — companies unsure about outcomes from outsourcing vendors
  • Pricing inconsistency — absence of industry benchmarks meant wide variation in vendor pricing
  • Security and ethics — concern over data breaches and vendor accountability

These were not simply observations of hesitancy — they were a precise diagnosis of what the industry needed to resolve before HR outsourcing could achieve mainstream adoption in India.

Historical Significance

A New Buzzword That Became Standard Practice

What was described in 2004 as a "new buzzword" has today become a fundamental element of modern business operations. From start-ups to multinational corporations, organisations routinely depend on specialised HR partners for:

Recruitment Process Outsourcing (RPO) Executive Search Payroll Administration Background Verification HR Technology Platforms Learning & Development Shared Services Published 2004

This Express India article is among the earliest mainstream discussions on the future of HR outsourcing in India — published at the precise moment when the industry was first beginning to take shape.
