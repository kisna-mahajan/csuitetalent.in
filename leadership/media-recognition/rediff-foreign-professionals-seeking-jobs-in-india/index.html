Context

When India Became a Destination, Not Just a Source

For decades, the narrative around India and talent was one of outflow — skilled professionals leaving for the United States, the United Kingdom, and other developed economies. By early 2004, Anil Mahajane was observing something different at first hand: professionals from those very countries were now calling to ask about opportunities in India.

This Rediff feature, published on 11 February 2004, was among the earliest online articles to document this emerging reversal in global talent flows. Enquiries were coming not only from the US and UK, but also from Germany, Italy, African nations — and they were increasing week on week.

From the Interview

Observations from Anil Mahajane

"Until sometime back it was only expatriate Indians who were shifting to places like Bangalore, Mumbai or New Delhi. However, for the last few months, overseas workers have also started showing interest in India and every week the number of queries from foreign professionals is increasing. It is not just because of the feel-good factor." — Anil Mahajane, Rediff, February 2004
"Moreover, not many countries offer benefits like political stability, comfort level and competence at the same time. Besides, countries like Singapore and Hong Kong — which were earlier attracting a lot of foreign workers — are now reaching saturation point. This stagnancy factor has reduced the number of opportunities available there." — Anil Mahajane, Rediff, February 2004
"Most inquiries are for middle- and senior-level management positions, but people are interested in lower rank jobs too." — Anil Mahajane, Rediff, February 2004
Sectors of Interest

Where Foreign Professionals Were Looking

The article noted that foreign professionals were exploring opportunities across a wide range of sectors — spanning both technology-led and traditional industries:

Information Technology
Biotechnology
Research & Development
Garment Industry
Hospitality

Enquiries were primarily for middle and senior management positions, though interest extended to a range of levels — a sign that India's appeal was broad rather than limited to the executive tier.

A Structural Shift

India's Unique Combination of Stability and Opportunity

Anil Mahajane identified a specific reason why India stood apart from other emerging market destinations. Singapore and Hong Kong — long favoured by international professionals — were approaching saturation. The opportunities available there were narrowing.

India offered something different: a combination of political stability, cultural comfort for returning diaspora, and rapidly expanding opportunities across both established and emerging sectors. That combination, he observed, was rare globally.

Historical Significance

A Prediction Borne Out Over Two Decades

When this article was published, the idea of foreign professionals actively seeking employment in India was still novel. Over the following two decades, the trend Anil Mahajane described quietly became the norm.
